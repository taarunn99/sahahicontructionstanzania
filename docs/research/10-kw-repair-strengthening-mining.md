# 10 - Keyword universe and SERP validation: concrete repair, grouts, anchoring, admixtures, structural strengthening, mining and underground (Tanzania / East Africa)

Research date: 2026-11-05. Client: Sahihi Construction Chemicals Limited, Dar es Salaam (sahihicc.com). Research only, nothing built.

## 0. Read this first: method and limits

**What was done.** 24 live web searches were run and their result lists read (domain and page type per result), then about 30 ranking or reference pages were fetched directly to verify facts (Sika Tanzania, MC-Bauchemie, Normet, The Citizen, TanzaniaInvest, AngloGold Ashanti, Byrnecut, Wikipedia, a World Bank contract-award listing, a Barrick tender advert).

**Limits that change how far you can trust this report.**

1. **The search budget ran out at 24 searches.** The brief asked for 40+ searches and 30+ graded SERPs. The session's shared search allowance (200 calls across all 14 agents) was exhausted after my 24th call. Section 3 therefore grades 24 SERPs, not 30+. Every query in section 2 says whether its SERP was checked ("Y") or not ("N"). Grades on unchecked queries are marked "inferred" and are my reasoning from neighbouring checked SERPs, not observations.
2. **The search tool is US-located.** It does not return google.co.tz results. A searcher in Dar es Salaam will see more jiji.co.tz, local directories and Swahili pages than I did. Treat every grade as "composition of an English-language, non-localised SERP". Re-check the top 30 queries from a Tanzanian IP or in Google Search Console once the site is live.
3. **No People Also Ask data.** The tool returns organic links only, no PAA boxes. I recorded zero PAA questions. Section 4 gives question-form titles copied verbatim from ranking pages instead, and labels everything else as candidates.
4. **No search-volume tool.** No volumes are stated anywhere. Priority is argued from SERP composition and lead value only.
5. **Mapei product facts were not re-verified.** mapei.com and utt.mapei.com returned HTTP 403 to every fetch. Product roles below (thickness ranges, crack suitability) come from the project brief, not from a datasheet I read today. Anything that would be published as a specification must be checked against the current Mapei TDS.
6. **sahihicc.com is currently a Namecheap parking page** (fetched 2026-11-05: "Your website is ready to go!"). A search for the brand name returned no page about the company. The new site starts with no indexed content and no observable brand SERP. Source: https://sahihicc.com

## 1. The findings that should change decisions

1. **Local-modifier queries in this line are wide open.** For "chemical anchor Tanzania" the result list contained no page about anchoring products at all (chemical-import news, UNEP, a safari brand). For "structural strengthening Tanzania CFRP" and "epoxy crack injection concrete Tanzania Dar es Salaam" not one Tanzanian result appeared. For "concrete repair mortar suppliers Dar es Salaam" 8 of the first 13 results were jiji.co.tz category pages. A competent category page per cluster has no local page to beat.
2. **Sika is the only competitor with real Tanzanian search presence, and it ranks with PDFs and thin pages.** Sika Tanzania PDFs held positions 1-2 for the crystalline admixture, polysulphide and shotcrete accelerator queries. Its HTML category pages are short: "Concrete Repair & Protection" is roughly 250-300 words with no FAQs or how-to; "Grouting & Fixing" is roughly 200-250 words and features one product. Sources: https://tza.sika.com/en/construction/concrete-repair-protection.html and https://tza.sika.com/en/construction/grouting-fixing.html
3. **"Mining chemicals Tanzania" is the wrong query to chase.** Its SERP is reagents and explosives: Orica (sodium cyanide), a Buckreef EOI for "Chemical and Reagents to Process Plant", Ideal Explosives, processing-chemical traders. The mining page should target "construction chemicals for mining", shotcrete, injection and underground waterproofing terms and only disambiguate "mining chemicals" in one H2.
4. **Mine sales are procurement-gated, not search-gated.** Bulyanhulu's 2025 shotcrete EOI required an approved Local Content Plan and a BRELA certificate proving Tanzanian ownership. The January 2026 reserved list for 100 percent Tanzanian-owned companies includes "Supply of Cement and Building Materials", "Supply of Underground Support Materials" and "Supply of Chemicals and Reagents". If Sahihi qualifies, that is the strongest message on the mining page; if it does not, the page should target the mines' contractors instead. Sahihi's ownership status is not known to me. Sources in section 6.
5. **Four stock gaps sit under high-value queries.** The brief's stock list has no general-purpose non-shrink cementitious grout, no epoxy machinery grout, no plasticizer or superplasticizer, and no CFRP system or shotcrete accelerator. Queries G1-G5, A4-A5, S2 and M3-M8 should only get dedicated pages if Sahihi can actually supply those lines to order. This needs a yes/no from the client before the page plan is frozen.

### 2.10 Geo variants and Swahili

**Geo variants (apply to queries 1, 19, 38, 43, 57, 68).** Not SERP-checked individually. Recommended handling: do not create city doorway pages. That is not confirmed.

**Swahili.** I could not verify any Swahili query in a live SERP (budget). The terms below are my own translations and are NOT verified as real search behaviour. Have a native speaker and Search Console data confirm before building anything on them:
- ukarabati wa zege (concrete repair)

- dawa ya nyufa (crack "treatment", colloquial)
- kuzuia maji kwenye zege (waterproofing concrete)

Working assumption, also unverified: engineers and procurement staff search in English; homeowners and small fundis may search in Swahili. If confirmed, Swahili belongs in 2-3 journal posts (crack repair, bonding old to new), not in the B2B category pages.

### 6.3 Coastal chloride and sulphate exposure

- General mechanism (sourced): chlorides cause pitting corrosion of reinforcement; "Concrete exposed to seawater is susceptible to its corrosive effects"; sulphates in solution react with cement phases and the resulting ettringite formation causes expansion and cracking (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_degradation).
- Local evidence (sourced): the Dar port concrete-damage repair contract above.
- Draft Tanzanian standard on repairing water-leakage cracks in concrete, adopting ISO/TR 16475, exists at TBS (https://www.tbs.go.tz/uploads/publications/en-1613397341-BCDC%201%20(33)%20DTZS.pdf; snippet only, PDF returned 404 on fetch; current status not verified).
- **NOT verified:** I found no measured chloride or sulphate data for Dar es Salaam, Tanga, Mtwara or Zanzibar soils and groundwater. The argument "coastal Tanzania needs sulphate-resistant repair mortar" is sound engineering reasoning (seawater and saline groundwater carry both chlorides and sulphates, so a patch made with ordinary mortar can fail before the parent concrete), but the site should present it as reasoning plus the port example, and should not quote local exposure numbers until a geotechnical or UDSM source is found.
- **NOT verified:** the November 2024 Kariakoo building collapse and any resulting building-audit programme. I could not retrieve a source today. Do not reference it in copy until sourced.

## 8. Interactive element: Repair Method Selector with crack-width gauge

**Why this one.** The checked SERPs contain product lists and thin category pages but no tool that turns a site observation into a product system. Sika's injection guide gives "no numeric crack widths or width-based selection criteria" (https://www.sika.com/en/knowledge-hub/injection-basics.html). A selector is the piece of content competitors do not have, and it produces a pre-qualified enquiry.

**Inputs (all user-driven, no free text needed until the end).**
1. Defect type: crack / spalled or delaminated concrete / honeycombing / exposed corroded rebar / void under a baseplate / construction or cold joint / leaking joint.
2. If crack: width (slider in mm with an on-screen gauge the user can hold a phone against, plus preset chips "hairline", "card-edge", "coin-edge"); is it moving (yes / no / not sure); condition (dry / damp / actively leaking).
3. If spall, honeycomb or void: repair depth in mm and area in m2; orientation (floor / wall / soffit).
4. Element: slab, beam, column, wall, tank or reservoir, foundation, tunnel lining.
5. Exposure: inland / coastal or marine / sulphate soil or sewage / underground.

**Output.**
- Recommended method in one sentence (inject, gravity-feed, rout and seal with flexible sealant, hand-applied mortar, poured microconcrete, grout).
- The stocked product system in order (for example: Mapefer 1K on cleaned rebar, bonding coat, Mapegrout T60, cure), with links to the product pages.
- Estimated quantity from depth and area.
- A "refer to a structural engineer" flag for structural cracks, moving cracks and anything in a column or beam.
- One button: "Send this to Sahihi on WhatsApp" with the inputs and result pre-filled as the message.

**Logic sketch (to be confirmed against Mapei TDS before build; thresholds are NOT verified).**
- Dormant, dry, fine cracks: low-viscosity epoxy injection (Epojet LV). An ACI bulletin recommends low-viscosity epoxy of 500 cps or less for cracks of 0.3 mm or smaller (https://www.concrete.org/Portals/0/Files/PDF/RAP-1.pdf, snippet). The exact upper width for Epojet LV must come from its datasheet.
- Wider dormant cracks in horizontal surfaces and construction joints: gravity-fed epoxy (Eporip).
- Moving cracks: do not use rigid epoxy; rout and seal with a flexible sealant (X-Seal PG) and refer to an engineer.
- Leaking cracks: outside the stocked epoxy range unless a water-reactive injection resin is available; show an honest "contact us" result.
- Spalls with exposed rebar: Mapefer 1K, then repair mortar; choose Mapegrout T60 where exposure is coastal, sulphate or sewage.
- Deep sections and jacketing (brief states 30-250 mm): Mapegrout ME05, formed and poured.
- Bond coat between old and new: Planicrete SP or X-Tech Bond SBR slurry for non-structural work, Eporip for structural joints.
