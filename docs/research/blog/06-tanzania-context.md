Research date: 2026-11-06. Subject: Sahihi Construction Chemicals Limited, sahihicc.com.
Cluster: the facts that make posts local (climate, regulators, standards, approvals) and the local informational searches (English and Swahili).

## 1. Method and limits

**What was done.** 18 web searches (the cap), all US-located. About 45 pages and documents were then fetched directly, with preference for primary sources: Tanzania Meteorological Authority (TMA) PDFs, the Contractors Registration Board (CRB) site and its 2022 by-laws, the Tanzania Bureau of Standards (TBS) compulsory standards list, the Urban Planning Act on the Ministry of Lands site, ERB, AQRB and NCC pages, and peer-reviewed or university papers. PDFs were downloaded and read as text; two CRB tables that exist only as images were read by eye.

**Limits that matter.**

1. **No search volumes and no Tanzanian SERPs.** Results are what a US-located search returned on 2026-11-06. "Who ranks" means "what that search returned", in no guaranteed order. Demand for every query is unmeasured. Statements about demand are marked *inference*.
2. **World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal could not be read.** `climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org` returned HTTP 403 to every request, including the country profile PDF. No figure in this report comes from it. A University of Cape Town climate profile of Dar es Salaam (published on gov.uk) and TMA's own annual climate statement are used instead.
3. **TanzLII returned HTTP 403**, so the 1999 Contractors Registration By-Laws were not read there. The 2022 amendment by-laws and the CRB pages were read on crb.go.tz instead. The Contractors Registration Act itself was not opened; section numbers quoted below are as CRB's own page states them.
4. **The Tausi portal (building permits) is a JavaScript application.** Only its title could be read. Building permit steps, documents, fees and timelines are therefore **not verified** and none is stated here.
5. **Springer, Wiley and MDPI blocked direct fetches.** One paper was read in full through Europe PMC (open access); two are cited from the abstract only, and this is marked.
6. **Three old TBS file URLs returned 404** (the 2019 gazetted list and the 2026 draft ISO 13007 files found by search). The current compulsory list (October 2026) was read instead and supersedes them for this purpose.
7. **Summaries by the fetch tool.** Several pages were read through a tool that paraphrases. Where an exact wording matters (CRB penalties, TMA season definitions, TBS entries, the Urban Planning Act) the text was pulled raw and is quoted from the raw text. Items read only as a paraphrase are marked "(paraphrase)".
8. **The AQRB site looks compromised.** On 2026-11-06 its home page and FAQ carried a third-party gambling-style link ("JURAGAN805"). Report 13 saw the same on 2026-11-05. AQRB facts below are limited to what its own pages state; do not link to its FAQ page from the site until it is clean.

**Rule applied.** No rule, class, fee or figure appears below unless it was read on an official or clearly reliable page on 2026-11-06. Everything else is in section 2.6, "Not verified".

**Outline**

2. What humidity does: slow drying of screeds and renders, condensation on cool slabs, the 75% application limit on resin floors (D12), shortened shelf life of bagged products (D12). Humidity through the day (D7) and why the hygrometer decides, not the calendar.
3. What heat does: shorter pot life and open time, skinning of adhesive in sun and wind, thermal movement of external tiling (link to Journal post 9, "Why tiles lift").
4. What salt and carbonation do to reinforced concrete: the port evidence (D8), the campus evidence (D9). Honest statement: a coating slows ingress; it does not replace cover and dense concrete.
5. Why paint peels on Dar es Salaam walls: rising damp and salts, the missing membrane under the slab (report 07 forum evidence, cited as what builders say, not as measurement).
6. Roofs and parapets under intense rain (D3, C9).
7. Zanzibar and Pemba: the same climate with more extreme daily rain (C9), and coral-stone buildings that need lime, not cement or epoxy (D10, D11); link to post 13.
8. A coast checklist for specifiers.

**Required facts and sources.** D1 to D12, C5, C9, C11. Do not print any chloride or carbonation number (2.6). Do not state service-life figures. Humidity limit quoted exactly from the data sheet, with the product named.

**Pages to link.** `/mapei/resin-flooring` and `mapefloor-fc-200-me`, `mapefloor-i-300-sl-me`, `primer-sn`; `/mapei/concrete-repair` and `mapefer-1k`, `mapegrout-t60`, `planicrete-sp`; `/mapei/waterproofing` and `antipluviol`, `mapelastic-smart`; `/mapei/primers-levelling/triblock-p`; `/x-calibur`; planned `/solutions/concrete-repair`, `/solutions/epoxy-flooring`, `/where-we-supply/zanzibar`.

**Data table.** Season (Jan-Feb, Mar-May, Jun-Sep, Oct-Dec) with mean maximum, mean minimum and rainfall for Dar es Salaam (D2, D4), source named in the caption; beside it the data sheet limits that each season tests.

**Diagram.** Section through a reinforced concrete beam near the sea: carbonation front and chlorides moving in, rust expanding, cover spalling, in four frames.

**Interactive.** "Can I apply today?" The reader enters air temperature, relative humidity and slab temperature; the page computes the dew point (Magnus formula, stated on the page) and shows whether the slab is at least the margin above it that the selected product's data sheet requires, plus the 75% check where the data sheet has one. Limits are read from `src/content/mapei/<slug>.json`; where a data sheet gives no figure the tool says so. A 3D element is not needed here.

### 5.6 Ujenzi Glossary: Swahili Building Terms in English

**Outline**

2. People: fundi, mkandarasi, mbunifu majengo, mkadiriaji majenzi, mhandisi.
3. Papers: ramani, makadirio, kibali cha ujenzi, hati, stika.

5. Materials: saruji, mchanga, kokoto, nondo, zege, tofali, bati, mbao, vigae, rangi.
6. Units heard at the counter: mfuko, tripu, fuso.
7. Problems: kuvuja, unyevu, kubanduka, nyufa (marked as unverified until the counter staff confirm).
8. Counting floors: ghorofa moja is two storeys (R8).
9. Seasons: Masika, Vuli, Msimu (C1 to C3).
10. Words that stay English on site: waterproof, tiles, cement, grout, skimming.

**Required facts and sources.** Section 6 below. Each entry needs one line of evidence or a sign-off by a Swahili-speaking member of staff; machine translation is not acceptable (report 13 section 2.4). **Data table.** The glossary itself: Swahili, English, where you meet it, related page. Marked up as a definition list so each term has an anchor (`/blog/<slug>#lenta`).

**Diagram.** One labelled house section with every stage word placed on the part it names, Swahili first, English beneath.

**Interactive.** A search-as-you-type filter over the list, and a Swahili-first or English-first toggle. The labelled house can respond to a tap on a term by highlighting the part. No 3D needed.
