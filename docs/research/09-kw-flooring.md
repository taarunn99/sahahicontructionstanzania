# 09 - Flooring keyword universe and SERP validation (resin, car park, sports, resilient)

Research date: 2026-11-05
Client: Sahihi Construction Chemicals Limited, Dar es Salaam (sahihicc.com)
Scope: epoxy, PU and PU-cement, car-park, screeds, sports and SPC/LVT flooring queries for Tanzania and East Africa
Status: research only. Nothing was built.

---

## 1. Method and limits (read this first)

**What was done.** 25 live web searches were run and their result lists recorded (21 keyword queries and 4 competitor-discovery queries). About 75 ranking or reference pages were then requested directly (competitor sites, directories, regulator documents, manufacturer pages), of which roughly 45 returned readable content. Every claim below carries its source URL.

**Limits that affect how far you can trust the grades.**

1. **Search budget ran out.** The brief asked for 40+ searches. The session-wide search quota (shared by all 14 agents) was exhausted after my 25th query, and 7 further queries returned no results. Queries I could not check live are marked **NOT CHECKED** or **INFERRED** in every table. I did not work around the limit.
2. **The search tool is US-located and is not Google Tanzania.** Result order approximates, but does not equal, what a buyer in Dar es Salaam sees on google.co.tz. Local packs (Google Business Profile map results), Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp Business listings are not visible to this tool. Applicators in Tanzania may be far more visible there than this report suggests.
3. **No search-volume tool.** No volumes are quoted anywhere. Demand is validated only by SERP composition (who bothers to rank), marketplace category structure, and regional competitor behaviour. Treat demand as "evidenced" or "unverified", never as a number.
4. **People Also Ask boxes were not retrievable.** The tool does not return PAA. Section 5 instead records, verbatim, the question headings and FAQ questions found on pages that rank for these queries. They are labelled as such and are not presented as Google PAA.
5. **Several sources blocked fetching** (HTTP 403): mapei.com product pages, jiji.co.tz and jiji.co.ke category pages, smartware.co.tz. Mapei data came from the cms.mapei.com mirror, which exposes the product description but not the technical data sheet values. Anything that needs a TDS figure is marked **NOT VERIFIED - take from TDS**.
6. **Grades are provisional.** EASY / MEDIUM / HARD describes how weak or strong the observed results were, for a new domain with good on-page content. Re-check the top 15 queries on a Tanzanian IP before committing budget.

**Two findings outside the brief that matter.**

- **sahihicc.com currently serves a Namecheap parking page** ("Your website is ready to go!"). The domain has no visible content equity today, so every ranking below must be earned from zero. Source: https://sahihicc.com (fetched 2026-11-05).
- **Product-copy discrepancy to resolve before writing pages.** The brief describes both Mapefloor Finish 415 ME and Finish 450 ME as aliphatic PU finishes. Mapei's UAE product page describes Finish 415 ME as a "Two-component, aromatic coloured, flexible, wear-resistant polyurethane finish" (https://cms.mapei.com/ae/en/products-and-solutions/products/detail/mapefloor-finish-415-me) and Finish 450 ME as a "Two-component, aliphatic, elastic coloured polyurethane finish resistant to wear and UV rays" (https://cms.mapei.com/ae/en/products-and-solutions/products/detail/mapefloor-finish-450-me). Aromatic PU yellows under UV, so this changes which product is recommended for exposed top decks and outdoor courts. Confirm against the TDS.

## 2. Headline findings

1. **No Tanzanian company page ranked for any of the 21 keyword queries checked.** Results were South African trade news, an empty directory, Jiji classifieds, Kenyan and Ugandan contractors, and global manufacturer pages. Example: for "epoxy flooring Tanzania" the top results were a 2019 South African news story about the airport, a GoAfrica directory page that reports "0 results", and Kenyan service directories. Sources: https://www.kznindustrialnews.co.za/2019/07/31/kzn-company-adds-polish-to-new-tanzania-airport-terminal/ , https://www.goafricaonline.com/tz/directory/coatings-surface-finishes , https://bestcareservices.co.ke/?p=35359
2. **The strongest local domain, Sika Tanzania, has thin flooring content.** Its flooring page carries roughly 250 words of substantive text and its industrial flooring page lists three products (Sikafloor-161, -263 SL, -264) with no specifications, no PU-cement, car-park or sports content in the page body. Sources: https://tza.sika.com/en/construction/flooring.html , https://tza.sika.com/en/construction/flooring/industrial-flooring.html
3. **Kenya and Uganda are a content generation ahead.** Kenyan sites publish price ranges, FAQs and sector posts; Jiji Kenya has a dedicated "Epoxy Flooring" category (https://jiji.co.ke/165-epoxy-flooring) while Jiji Tanzania only has generic "Flooring Materials" and "Flooring Services" (https://jiji.co.tz/165-flooring-materials , https://jiji.co.tz/142-flooring-services). A Kampala firm already publishes a Tanzania landing page with a Swahili section (https://shop.uelresins.com/fibreglass-and-resin-materials-supplier-in-tanzania). If Sahihi does not take the Tanzanian SERPs, a Nairobi or Kampala firm will.
4. **Nairobi applicators already name Dar es Salaam as a market.** Flexo Concepts lists "Tanzania (Dar es Salaam)" among countries served (https://epoxyresinflooring.co.ke/) and an older Nairobi blog lists Dar es Salaam too (https://industrialflooringkenya.blogspot.com).
5. **Tanzanian applicators are effectively invisible in web search.** None surfaced with their own site. That is the distributor's opening: an "approved applicator" programme page captures "contractor" intent that Sahihi cannot serve directly, and gives applicators a reason to buy Mapei from Sahihi.
6. **Regulatory hooks are real and citable.** TMDA's GMP inspection guideline (3rd edition, July 2025) bases requirements on WHO TRS 986 Annex 2, which requires floors that are "smooth and free from cracks and open joints". This is the strongest factual anchor for pharma flooring content. Sources in section 7.

| Dar es Salaam: tropical savanna climate. Mean daily maximum 29.7 to 32.8 C across the year; mean daily minimum 19.1 to 24.9 C (1991-2020 normals, Julius Nyerere International Airport). | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam |

**What this means for content (my inference from the figures above, to be confirmed against Mapei TDS):** ambient humidity in Dar es Salaam sits at or above the 75 percent threshold FeRFA applies to epoxy substrates for most of the year; slab temperatures of 30 C and more shorten working time and overcoating windows; the April to May and November to December rains are the high-risk months for new ground slabs and exposed decks. That supports three content angles no competitor uses: moisture testing before coating, scheduling pours and coatings around the rains, and why PU-cement tolerates damp substrates better than epoxy.

### 7C. Hygiene and regulatory expectations for floors

**Pharmaceutical (TMDA).** TMDA's "Guidelines for Good Manufacturing Practices Inspection of Human Medicinal Products Manufacturing Facilities, Third Edition, July 2025" states that its requirements "are based on WHO Technical Report Series (TRS) documents" and names WHO TRS 986 Annex 2 as the main-principles reference. Source: https://www.tmda.go.tz/publications/40 (document listed there; text read from the PDF).

WHO TRS 986 Annex 2, clause 12.27: "Where starting and primary packaging materials and intermediate or bulk products are exposed to the environment, interior surfaces (walls, floors and ceilings) should be smooth and free from cracks and open joints, should not shed particulate matter, and should permit easy and effective cleaning and, if necessary, disinfection." Clause 12.29: "Drains should be of adequate size and designed and equipped to prevent back-flow. Open channels should be avoided where possible, but if they are necessary they should be shallow to facilitate cleaning and disinfection." Source: https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/trs986-annex2

Near-identical wording appears in EU GMP Chapter 3, clause 3.9 (https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/chapter_3_0.pdf). The TMDA guideline also lists "Poor cleaning procedures of the manufacturing equipment and premises" among its examples of critical non-compliances (Table 1), and TMDA publishes an "Action Plan to Promote Domestic Medical Products Manufacturing Facilities 2022/23-2026/27" (https://www.tmda.go.tz/publications/40).

Content rule: say that seamless resin floors help a facility meet clause 12.27. Do not say a product is "TMDA approved" or "GMP certified"; no such product approval was found.

**Food and beverage.** Codex General Principles of Food Hygiene, section 4.2.2: "the surfaces of walls, partitions and floors should be made of impervious materials with no toxic effect in intended use" and "floors should be constructed to allow adequate drainage and cleaning". Source: https://www.fao.org/4/y1579e/y1579e02.htm (1997 revision, amended 1999; a later revision exists and should be checked for current numbering).

NOT VERIFIED: any Tanzania Bureau of Standards (TBS) standard that sets floor-finish requirements for food premises. I found no TBS document and could not search for one. Do not cite TBS on floors until someone confirms a standard number.

## 9. Interactive element: Floor System Builder (layer explorer)

**Why this fits.** No page fetched in this research, including Sika Tanzania and Flowcrete's car-park range page, shows an interactive build-up; Flowcrete's page has "No system build-up diagrams, layer descriptions, or technical tables" (https://www.flowcrete.eu/en-gb/our-products/product-ranges/car-park-decking/). Resin flooring is sold as layered systems, and buyers arrive not knowing the layers. A tool that turns their situation into a named build-up is the most direct route from visit to WhatsApp enquiry on a site with no prices.

**Inputs (the visitor chooses):**
1. Use: food or beverage wet process / pharmaceutical or clean area / warehouse or dry production / workshop / car park intermediate deck / car park exposed top deck / showroom or office / sports court.
2. Traffic: foot / pallet truck / forklift / vehicles.
3. Exposure: dry / occasional spills / chemicals or hot wash-down / outdoor sun and rain.
4. Substrate: new slab (age in weeks) / old concrete / existing tiles; optional moisture reading (percent RH).
5. Area in m2.

**Outputs (computed on the page):**
- An exploded cross-section, each layer clickable: substrate -> primer -> body coat -> broadcast aggregate -> finish, with product name and thickness per layer and total system thickness. Example: Primer SN -> Mapefloor I 300 SL ME at 2 to 4 mm -> Mapefloor Finish 450 ME.
- A plain-language reason for the recommendation and the resin flooring type it corresponds to (FeRFA types 1 to 8, from https://www.ferfa.org.uk/standard/).
- Warnings triggered by inputs: moisture reading above the limit for the chosen resin; slab younger than the product allows; exposed deck with a non-UV-stable topcoat.
- An indicative material take-off in kilograms per product for the entered area, calculated from TDS consumption rates.
- A "Send this build-up on WhatsApp" button that pre-fills the selections, area and city into the message. This is the lead capture, and it arrives pre-qualified.

**Data rules.** Layer thicknesses, consumption rates and moisture and temperature limits must be loaded from the Mapei and X-Calibur technical data sheets. None were retrievable in this research, so no rates are proposed here. Every output should carry "indicative - confirm with Sahihi technical team".

**Reuse.** The same component, filtered to one system, sits on each system page and each product page ("where this product sits in the build-up"), which also creates consistent internal links.
