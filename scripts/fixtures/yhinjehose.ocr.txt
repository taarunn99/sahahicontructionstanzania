YHInjehose
Re-injectable Hose for Sealing Construction Joints
Technical Date Sheet (TDS) 1 / 3
DESCRIPTION
YHInjectube is a re-injectable hose for sealing various construction and connection joints in watertight
concrete structures. To seal the joint, YHInjectube is injected with suitable Injection materials, such as
acrylate and microfine cement suspensions (for multiple injections) or polyurethane and epoxy resins (for
single injection). With the integrated "valve techniques" the hose can be used to re-inject multiple times if
necessary.
WHERE TO USE
YHInjectube is used to prevent water ingress through concrete joints in the following typical structures:
Water retaining or excluding structures, water reservoirs, dams, canals, sewage treatment plants,
tunnels, subways, retaining walls. It is suitable for many different structures and construction methods
where additional joint security/safety is required. It can be used as a back-up system for waterstops.

Substrate preparation: Concrete surface must be clean and all loose materials removed before installing
hose and placing concrete.
Injection Procedure: Locate junction boxes which house appropriate vent ends for each designated hose
segment. Open junction box to expose hose vent ends. The hose vent ends shall be terminated using clear
and green non-perforated PVC hose. The color coded vent ends help identify which hose is being injected.
For each individual hose segment, the two ends shall be terminated with a different color vent hose. For
example, after opening a junction box and exposing the vent ends, if you select a colored vent end to inject,
the opposite end of that hose segment should be terminated with a clear vent hose. If the applicator is
unsure of what vent end corresponds with what hose, check by pumping clean water through each hose
segment. Pumping clean water through the hose is highly suggested not only to identify the opposite vent
end, but also to check for any hose blockage or damage, or excessive voids in concrete that may lead to
excessive loss of injection resin during hose injection.
Insert the packer wrench into the injection vent end and connect pump to the packer wrench. The pump
hose assembly should be terminated with a sliding clutch (button head coupling). The sliding clutch will
slide over the "button head" fitting on the sliding clutch to connect the pump to the sliding clutch.
Prepare the injection material per the manufacturer's instructions and pour into injection hopper
connected to the pump. Once the connection is made, start the pump and begin injecting hose until
material flows out the opposite end (vent end). Injection can be successfully performed at injection
pressures as low as 75 - 150 psi. However, it will be observed that most conventional pumps have a
minimum output of approximately 300 psi. Therefore, you will find that the injection will have to be
performed at pressures closer to 300 psi or greater. Higher injection pressures will not compromise the
integrity of waterstop, but it is suggested to keep injection pressures below 600 psi to prevent
cross-contamination of adjacent waterstop, when using multiple rows of hose within a single joint.
When the injection material is observed flowing out the opposite end of the hose, stop the pump (or close
the ball valve on the pump assembly). Close off the vent end with another packer wrench tool. Once the
vent end is securely closed off with the packer wrench, start the pump and continue to inject the injection
materials into the hose at a pressure between 100 and 600 psi. Slow, low-pressure injections are more
effective than rapid, high-pressure injections. A successful injection will be indicated by the pump's
refusal of injection resin (the piston will quit pumping) or by the sight of injection materials seeping along
the length of the joint. At that time, hold the pressure between 500 and 600 psi for approximately 2 more
minutes.
Once the hose segment is successfully injected per the previously described criteria, disconnect the pump
and hook it up to the packer wrench at the opposite hose segment end, leaving the packer wrench
plugged into the end that was just injected. Re-inject the hose from the opposite end to ensure that the
entire joint has been filled. In most cases, the joint will already be successfully sealed and the pump will
refuse to inject any additional material. Use the previously described procedure/criteria for injecting and
determining successful joint injection on this end of the hose as well.
This procedure must be completed within the specified gel time of the material, yet still allowing time for
vacuuming and rinsing operations before the injection materials.
Turn off the pump, disconnect the pump hose from the packer wrench, and remove both packer wrench
from the hose segment.

is at least 48 months under normal transportation and storage conditions.
